# Alternative Plots

**None of this is shipped, and none of it is proposed either.** `docs/lore.md` is the
campaign; this file is three campaigns it could have been. Nothing here should be planned
against, quoted in a brief, or treated as furniture — the whole point of the exercise is
that the machinery underneath is stronger than the story currently mounted on it.

The baseline each of these holds to:

- Mars, one canyon, no ground logistics — everything arrives from orbit.
- The player is the bellhop from *Four Rooms*: present in every room, party to none of it.
  No cockpit, no seat, no name that means anything.
- Three parties with distinct principles, one airframe and one control scheme each.
- Prologue: land the antenna. Epilogue: fall past your own colony, in pieces.

And the machinery each of them reuses unchanged: 30 drops, the silent relay, the naming
axis (what each client calls you does more characterisation than anything they say),
depth as the faction axis, colony grown from completed deliveries, a corridor that closes
because you closed it.

---

## 1. The Salvage — *the canyon is already dead*

**Pitch.** Nothing is being founded here. Coprates Chasma held a colony that failed eleven
years ago, and three parties have come back for it. You rebuild it a piece at a time, on
top of itself, for people who each need a different answer about why it fell.

| | Party | Voice | Wants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Floor | **Ixion Recovery** — two survivors of the original crew, back on someone else's contract | named human, warm, and never once says what they were doing here before | the bodies and the logs |
| West wall | **Helion Underwriting** — the insurer who paid out and now owns the wreck | claims-adjustment forms, one file number, amended | to establish the cause was operator error, because the alternative is a design defect it also insured |
| East deep | **Kessler Deep** — the decommissioning contractor hired to make it safe | a foreman who cuts up dead machines for a living and talks to them | the deep levels sealed before anyone reads them |

**Depth is chronology.** The colony was built downward as it got poorer, so the shaft is a
timeline: the floor is the funded years, −58 is the year they cut the crew, −300 is the
last eight weeks. Every descent is further back into the reason.

**The arc.** 1–4 relay and radar, and the four dark relays on the floor are now *evidence*
rather than atmosphere. 5–9 the two charters take the wall and the shaft. 10–14 Helion
needs the record to say one thing and Ixion's recovered logs say another; you carry both,
on alternating runs, to opposite addresses. 15–21 Kessler starts backfilling levels ahead
of the survey — always legitimately, always slightly early. 22–27 the old workings and the
new ones share one pillar, because the salvage is re-entering a stope the original colony
abandoned for a reason nobody looked up. 28 Ixion finds what they came for and stops
transmitting mid-recovery. 30 Kessler's Final Charge is the seal he was contracted for on
day one.

**The epilogue writes itself.** The second collapse is the first collapse, repeated, by
three parties who each held one third of the reason and filed it separately. You fall past
a colony that has now fallen twice, and both times you built it.

**Never answered:** whether Ixion's survivors were the cause.

**Cost:** dialogue only. Same targets, same props, same budgets.

## 2. The Culture — *the colony is alive, and someone owns it*

**Pitch.** The thing in the rock is not ore, it is a substrate — and the structures you are
building are not built, they are grown. This is the version that makes
`docs/plans/mycelial_colony_growth.md` diegetic: the sprawl that appears between missions
stops being production and becomes the plot.

| | Party | Voice | Principle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Floor | **Ixion Outpost** — found it, named it, published it, kept nothing | named human, increasingly careful about verbs (*it spreads* versus *it decides*) | the record again — but now the record is a paper nobody will cite |
| West wall | **Helion Biotic** — holds the deposited strain and licenses it back | licensing instrument, machine-generated: `THE ORGANISM IS HELION'S BY PRIOR CULTURE` | ownership by registration |
| East deep | **Kessler Deep** — feedstock. It burns, it is local, it is free | a foreman who calls it *the crop* for eleven missions and never once calls it alive | tonnage |

**Depth is temperature.** It grows where it is warm, and warm is down. Helion cultivates a
controlled bed in a roofed cavern; Kessler chases the wild growth to 300 metres and finds
it thickening rather than thinning — which is the campaign's only real escalation and needs
no dialogue at all.

**The mechanic is already there.** Growth budget scales with how well you fly. Here that is
the story: fly well, feed it well, and by mission 25 the corridor you have to thread is the
thing you grew. Ixion's briefs track it quietly — sol counts, spread rates, the same figure
climbing — and read as botany or as something else, and the game never adjudicates.

**The pillar becomes the bed.** Two parties culturing the same organism from opposite sides,
one sterile and licensed, one industrial and hot, meeting in the middle where the rock is
now more growth than stone.

**Epilogue.** The Final Charge is a sterilisation, and what comes down is the colony's own
mass, because it was never load-bearing rock holding it up. Ixion's dark cut-in at 30 works
unchanged — the mast is still transmitting, and the second reading is worse.

**Never answered:** whether it responded to anything.

**Cost:** dialogue, plus an honest retune of `colonyBudget` so the growth reads as spread
rather than construction.

## 3. The Manifest — *the window closes and not everything is going home*

**Pitch.** The canyon is being abandoned. One transfer window, a fixed lift mass, three
parties bidding for it. You are the only one who physically moves anything, so every
argument about what is worth taking is settled by you, in kilograms, in your hands.

| | Party | Voice | Position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Floor | **Ixion Outpost** — eleven people and eleven years of cores | named human, still hospitable, quietly reweighing the archive against a person | wants the record home |
| West wall | **Helion Allocation** — the authority that publishes the manifest | allocation notices, revisions 1 → 11: `CONSIGNMENT NOT SCHEDULED. NO APPEAL LIES.` | mass is a number and the number is closed |
| East deep | **Kessler Deep** — still cutting, because ore has a price and people have a fare | a foreman who intends to make one more contract before the boat leaves | wants his last lift |

**This is the version where payload mass is the drama.** Every brief already carries a mass.
From about mission 14 the manifest's remaining allowance is printed on Helion's card and
visibly falling. Ixion's late cargo gets lighter run by run without comment — 1.0, then 0.6,
then 0.3 — which is a decision being made off-screen and never stated. Kessler's gets
heavier.

**Depth is desperation.** Helion's cavern is a bonded store: everything sealed there is
*staying*. Kessler goes deeper because the last seam has to pay three fares.

**The arc.** 1–9 as shipped. 10–14 the manifest opens and the corridor closes together.
15–21 you fly bonded goods *down* into the cavern — the only missions in the game where
delivering means abandoning. 22–27 the arbitration is a fare dispute. 28 Ixion ships out,
radar left powered, archive delivered *to the pad* rather than to orbit, which tells you
which way that decision went. 30 Kessler's last charge is a production blast on the wrong
side of the window.

**Epilogue.** Nobody detonates anything for a reason. The site was left, the pillar was
nobody's responsibility after 28, and you fall past a colony that came down because
everyone had already gone. Helion's `CARRIER: CLASSIFICATION PENDING` becomes literal — you
were never on a manifest.

**Never answered:** whether anyone made the launch.

**Cost:** dialogue, plus a mass allowance rendered on Helion's card — the only new UI in any
of the three.

## Comparison

- **Furthest from what is built:** 2, because it wants the growth system retuned.
- **Cheapest and most complete:** 1 — pure text, and it retroactively pays off the four dark
  relays that `docs/plans/mission_zero.md` already puts on the floor.
- **Best use of an existing mechanic nobody is dramatising yet:** 3. Payload mass currently
  affects handling and scoring and nothing else.
